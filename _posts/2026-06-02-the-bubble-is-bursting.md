# The Modern Dotcom Bubble

Outside of the shrinking group of AI evangelists, I think we're all starting to agree that AI has been the modern dotcom bubble. Even for those who believe it has a variety of powerful applications in an array of industries, the _true costs_ are beginning to reveal themselves as these AI companies start trying to become profitable. What once cost $20/month now costs $100. Token quotas that lasted a full month now last less than a week. Anyone familiar with the way startup tech companies price their products saw this coming; the price you paid a year ago was never going to be profitable for Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, or Microsoft. Those prices were intended to get you hooked and dependent on the product, and they were always going to squeeze you later on.

Just like the dotcom bubble burst of 2000, these are wildly unprofitable endeavors. They are burning through cash in quantities us normies simply cannot comprehend. OpenAI, for example, lit about $37 million dollars a day on fire in 2025. That's $1.5 million per hour, or about $430 per second. Imagine that: $430 dollars per second. **PER SECOND**. In the time it took you to read this paragraph, OpenAI burned another $4300 dollars.

OpenAI, of course, is not unique here. Anthropic recently announced filing for an IPO despite losing **$5.2 BILLION dollars** in 2025. Microsoft and Google are reportedly losing billions on their AI initiatives. Companies that have shifted to AI-first strategies and fired most of their developers are finding that users don't want their product, they can't keep their legacy products online, and that there simply isn't enough infrastructure in the US to support any of this.

The average person is turning on them. What was in 2024 a cute way to waste some time, get some surprisingly clever answers (to a layperson), or generate videos of Mickey Mouse doing the Harlem Shake is now the thing that cost them their job. It's the thing causing their property tax to skyrocket, their power costs to go through the roof, and their water quality to fall through the floor. It's the reason they can't buy a new computer, why they can't afford a PS5, and why Netflix is raising its price again.

The bubble is about to burst... and yet somehow the line keeps going up.

# King Nvidia

I'm not going to sit here and rehash the Gamers Nexus coverage of Nvidia's rise to dominance and their alignment with the current US government's authoritarian designs. If you want a painfully deep dive into just how deeply you're getting fucked, [their YouTube channel is over there](https://youtu.be/XJMbeG3tXjc?si=jc4ODDtWR5S-dJOh). No, instead I'm going to focus on talking about _why the AI bubble hasn't burst yet_, and why it might never. And it all revolves around Nvidia.

It's really weird to talk about Nvidia the same way people talk about Palantir or Lockheed Martin; you know, evil corporations getting in bed with the government to spend taxpayer money on moral atrocities. It wasn't that long ago that they were just a graphics card chipset company. Even as cryptocurrency mining exploded in 2020, Nvidia was - at least on paper - just making graphics cards mostly meant for video games. Sure, shareholders allege that they lied about how much they fed into that boom, but ultimately they were still producing video cards ostensibly _meant_ for consumers.

Suddenly, though, Nvidia is all but leaving the consumer market. In just the last two years, Nvidia has decided that its 33 year investment in the consumer graphics market is worthless. It discarded it like an old toy. Now, Nvidia makes AI chips. They've vertically integrated the entire pipeline such that they essentially have a monopoly on AI cards, because unlike their consumer video cards, they manufacture these entirely in-house. Some competitors exist, but as we see in the memory chip and storage chip markets, they're all colluding and investing in each other (strategies which, frankly, should be flagrantly illegal but somehow aren't). You've heard the saying "a rising tide lifts all boats," which is probably an attractive idiom for billionaires that own multiple yachts - but when _they_ say it, they're not talking about **you**. They're referring to **them**. To quote a now-tired George Carlin bit, ["it's a big club, and you ain't in it."](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/oLI0RuDAqjI)

> "But, Justin, you said the bubble is about to burst. Won't this leave Nvidia high and dry?"

Thanks for asking such a narratively convenient question, strawperson! **No.** That's the secret here - anyone with any sort of business sense or understanding of basic economics is going to look at the AI hype bubble and see an absolute flood of _supply_ but almost no _consumer demand_. They keep building these data centers, pushing out these models, monopolizing all the memory and storage chips for these oceans of servers... all to provide a product that the average consumer is starting to _hate_. But it's not about _consumer demand_ anymore.

What I'm about to describe can only happen because of unprecedented levels (at least in the last century) of wealth inequality. You don't matter anymore. There is so much money in the hands of the wealthy and so little in your hands that _they don't need you_. The companies and the billionaires and the ideologues behind this push have little interest in your $20 bucks. They want the billions-and-billions of dollars that the government will feed them without your input. They want to pass that money around their little circle over and over again, exploiting the broken-ass financial systems we've created to amplify their wealth, until the actual goal is within reach.

Nvidia wants to seize power and become the king of a digital feudalism, and they want you to live out the rest of your days as a serf.
